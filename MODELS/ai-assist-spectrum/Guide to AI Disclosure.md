# Guide to AI Disclosure

AI tools now participate in many forms of creative and technical work, but their involvement isn’t always visible from the final output. Clear disclosure helps readers understand how a piece was produced, preserves trust in authorship, and distinguishes human judgment from automated generation. It also aligns with emerging norms across academia, publishing, and industry, where transparency about AI assistance is increasingly expected. The goal isn’t to diminish the work, but to give audiences an honest view of the collaboration behind it.

---

## Level 0 — Fully human; no AI involvement  
No citation needed.  
(Historical default: authors claim full credit unless otherwise stated.)

---

## Level 1 — Human‑authored; AI refined wording or clarity  
**Refined with assistance of AI tools.**  
AI improved clarity, grammar, or expression without altering ideas or structure.

---

## Level 2 — Human‑authored; AI expanded the work

For full transparency, the recommended citation should reflect the **specific nature** of the AI’s contribution. Choose the phrasing that best matches how the AI extended your human‑authored structure:

- **Detail‑expanded with assistance of AI tools.**  
  Core ideas and structure were provided by the human; AI added detail, elaboration, examples, or connective explanation.

- **Structure‑expanded with assistance of AI tools.**  
  AI added sections, reorganized the flow, or scaffolded new structural framing while the human remained the primary author.

- **Content‑expanded with assistance of AI tools.**  
  AI contributed new ideas, conceptual material, or thematic breadth beyond the human‑provided base.

The above options disclose the involvement of AI in a transparent and narrowly scoped way. If the nature of the expansion is difficult to classify—or if the work involves multiple intertwined forms of expansion—a generic **Expanded with assistance of AI tools.** is acceptable. However, without being specific, this phrasing may imply a broader AI contribution.

---

## Level 3 — Human‑led; AI co‑developed through iteration or collaboration  
Choose based on the workflow:

- **Iteratively developed with assistance of AI tools.**  
  Human led direction; AI contributed through iterative drafts, variations, or refinement cycles.

- **Developed collaboratively with assistance of AI tools.**  
  Human remained primary author; AI contributed substantive material, but the process was not strictly iterative.

---

## Level 4 — AI‑led; human provided constraints  
**Generated with assistance of AI tools.**  
Human guided constraints or goals, but AI produced the majority of the work.

---

## Level 5 — Fully AI‑generated from minimal human input  
**Generated with assistance of AI tools.**
Human provided only a minimal prompt; AI generated the work.

---

Levels 4 and 5 both describe work where AI is the primary creator and the human is not the author in any meaningful legal or creative sense. Although the workflow differs—Level 4 involves human constraints, Level 5 involves minimal prompting—the outcome is the same: the human cannot claim authorship or copyright. For readers, publishers, and legal frameworks, this distinction offers no practical value. A single disclosure that the work was generated with AI assistance is sufficient, and further granularity would add complexity without improving transparency.

---

## Where to place AI disclosure

There are two common patterns for placing the disclosure, depending on the formality of the document.

### Informal placement (recommended for posts, memos, and lightweight documents)
Place the disclosure immediately after the title, right‑justified.
This keeps it visible without interrupting the reading flow.

Example:

```
Title of the Document
                        Refined with assistance of AI tools.
```
This pattern works well for Medium posts, internal notes, and short technical write‑ups.

### Formal placement (recommended for structured documents)
Create a dedicated section titled AI Disclosure, and place the disclosure phrase directly under it.

This section should appear:

- before any citation or reference section, or

- as the final section if the document does not include citations or references.

This approach mirrors how organizations handle metadata, revision history, and compliance notes.

Example structure:

```md
# Title of the Document

## Introduction
...

## AI Disclosure
Refined with assistance of AI tools.
```
This pattern is ideal for specifications, design docs, whitepapers, and long‑form technical writing.
