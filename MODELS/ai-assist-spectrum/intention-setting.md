# Expert Note: Intention‑Setting and the AI‑Assist Spectrum

Most people use the AI‑Assist Spectrum as a descriptive tool — a way to classify a piece of work after it’s finished. That’s perfectly valid, and for many creators it’s all that’s needed.

For expert users, educators, and teams who design repeatable workflows, there’s an additional layer worth considering: declaring your intended level of AI involvement at the start of a project. This isn’t required for using the Spectrum, and it’s not necessary for everyday work. But in environments where clarity, reproducibility, and shared expectations matter, intention‑setting can reduce ambiguity later on.

Stating an intended level early helps teams align on boundaries, understand how AI will participate, and interpret the final artifact in light of the process that produced it. It also makes evaluation more straightforward, since the criteria for authorship were known from the beginning rather than reconstructed after the fact.

This practice is optional and applies mainly to people who already think in terms of process design or collaborative norms. The Spectrum itself remains fully usable without it. But for those building systems, policies, or structured workflows, intention‑setting can serve as a quiet anchor that keeps authorship transparent and consistent across projects.
