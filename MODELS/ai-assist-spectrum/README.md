# AI‑Assist Spectrum
*A practical framework for understanding human authorship in the age of AI tools*

## Overview
As AI becomes a standard collaborator in creative and technical work, creators need a clear, shared way to describe **how much** AI contributed to a given artifact. The AI‑Assist Spectrum provides a simple model for classifying authorship across six levels, from fully human to fully AI‑generated.

This framework is **domain‑agnostic** and applies to writing, design, coding, architecture, and visual creation. To keep the levels concrete and comparable, each one is illustrated using the same example task: **creating a sunset scene**.

---

## Why This Spectrum Matters

This model gives creators, teams, and readers a shared vocabulary for describing AI involvement with clarity and transparency. It supports:
- ethical disclosure
- copyright awareness
- consistent communication
- clearer expectations for collaborators and audiences

As AI becomes a standard tool, the ability to articulate how it was used becomes just as important as the work itself.

---

# Level 0 — Fully Human (No AI Involvement)
**Definition:**  
The human creates the entire work manually, without any AI assistance.

**Example (Sunset):**  
A person paints, photographs, or writes a sunset scene entirely by hand.

**Human Creativity:**  
Complete.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
Yes — full human authorship.

---

# Level 1 — Human‑Authored, AI‑Refined
**Definition:**  
The human creates the full content; AI provides polishing, editing, or cleanup.

**Example (Sunset):**  
A human paints or photographs a sunset, then uses AI to adjust lighting, color balance, or clarity.

**Human Creativity:**  
Very high.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
Yes — AI refinement does not diminish human authorship.

---

# Level 2 — Human‑Authored, AI‑Expanded
**Definition:**  
The human provides the core creative structure; AI expands or elaborates on it.

**Example (Sunset):**  
A human sketches the composition of a sunset, and AI renders it into a detailed image.

**Human Creativity:**  
High — the human defines the structure and intent.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
Yes — for the human‑authored elements.

---

# Level 3 — Human‑Led, AI‑Co‑Created
**Definition:**  
The human directs the process through iterative refinement, selection, and evaluation. AI generates options; the human shapes the final outcome.

**Example (Sunset):**  
The human iterates through 10–20 AI‑generated variations, adjusting composition, lighting, and subjects, selecting and steering the final result.

**Human Creativity:**  
Moderate — the human makes meaningful creative decisions.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
Case‑by‑case — depends on whether the human’s contributions rise to the level of original creative expression.

---

# Level 4 — AI‑Led, Human‑Guided
**Definition:**  
The human provides constraints or descriptive prompts, but the AI determines the creative expression.

**Example (Sunset):**  
“Sunset at the beach, couple embracing, flowers nearby, birds in the distance.”

**Human Creativity:**  
Low — the human sets parameters, but the AI invents the scene.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
No — insufficient human authorship.

---

# Level 5 — Fully AI‑Generated
**Definition:**  
The AI creates the entire work based on a minimal prompt.

**Example (Sunset):**  
“Generate a sunset.”

**Human Creativity:**  
Minimal — the human only triggers the generation.

**Copyright Eligibility:**  
No — the work lacks human authorship.

## When a Work Is Not Copyrightable
Some levels of the AI‑Assist Spectrum (particularly Levels 4 and 5, and sometimes Level 3) do not meet the legal threshold for human authorship. When a work is not copyrightable:

- Anyone may use, share, modify, or redistribute it.  
- No permission is required.  
- No exclusive rights apply.  
- No license is needed because there is no copyright to license.

This does not necessarily place the work in the traditional “public domain,” which has a specific legal meaning, but the practical effect is similar: the work is free for anyone to use without restriction.

This distinction helps creators, teams, and audiences understand what rights apply at each level of AI involvement.
