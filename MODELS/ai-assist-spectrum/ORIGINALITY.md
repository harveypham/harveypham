# Originality and Prior Art

The AI‑Assist Spectrum is an original conceptual framework developed to classify human and AI contribution across creative and technical workflows. While adjacent ideas exist in fields such as AI disclosure policy, human‑in‑the‑loop research, and copyright guidance, none of these works define a structured, hierarchical authorship model or provide practical disclosure and evaluation methods.

Existing materials typically fall into one of the following categories:

- **AI disclosure rules** (e.g., academic and publisher guidelines)  
  These specify when AI use must be acknowledged but do not define levels of contribution or authorship semantics.

- **Human–AI interaction taxonomies** (e.g., mixed‑initiative systems, HCI research)  
  These describe interaction patterns, not authorship or creative contribution.

- **AI capability or task lists**  
  These enumerate what AI tools can do but do not form a hierarchical model of authorship.

- **Copyright and legal frameworks**  
  These distinguish human authorship from machine‑generated works but do not articulate intermediate levels or practical disclosure language.

The AI‑Assist Spectrum differs from all of these. It introduces:

- a **0–5 hierarchy** of authorship contribution  
- **clear definitions** for each level  
- **workflow‑based distinctions** between refinement, expansion, collaboration, and generation  
- **practical disclosure phrases** aligned with each level  
- a **method for evaluating** a work using an AI system  
- a **canonical structure** maintained in this repository

This combination does not appear in prior literature, policy, or tooling. The Spectrum, its definitions, its disclosure language, and its evaluation method constitute an original model authored for public use and reference.
