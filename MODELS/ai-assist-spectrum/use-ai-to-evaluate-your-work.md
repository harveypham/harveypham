# Evaluate Your Work Using the AI‑Assist Spectrum

This guide explains how to use an AI system to determine the appropriate authorship level for a piece of work according to the AI‑Assist Spectrum. The goal is to help you classify your process accurately, whether you are evaluating work within an ongoing AI conversation or starting fresh in a new session. The steps below outline how to provide the right context so the evaluation remains consistent, reliable, and aligned with the definitions in the main Spectrum guide.

## Instruction

The evaluation prompt is designed to classify a piece of work according to the AI‑Assist Spectrum. How you use it depends on whether the evaluation happens **within the same AI conversation** or **in a new session**.

### 1. Evaluating Within the Same Conversation
If the work was created in the current AI session, simply paste the evaluation prompt into the conversation.  
The AI already has full context of the interaction, drafts, refinements, and decision points, so no additional information is needed.

### 2. Evaluating in a New Conversation or Fresh Session
If you start a new session, the AI has no memory of prior steps. To evaluate accurately, you must provide context. There are two approaches:

#### **Option A — Provide the Full Conversation (Most Accurate)**
Paste:
- the key parts of the conversation where the work was developed  
- the final product  
- then paste the evaluation prompt  

This gives the AI the same visibility it would have had if the work were created in‑session.

#### **Option B — Provide a Summary or Essence (May Be Sufficient)**
You may provide:
- a **summary** of the development process, or  
- an **essence** capturing the major steps, decisions, and contributions  

This can be enough for a correct classification *if* the summary accurately reflects:
- who originated the ideas  
- how much the AI contributed  
- whether the AI added detail, structure, or content  
- whether the process was iterative  

However, summaries can omit subtle but important signals (e.g., who initiated conceptual moves), so accuracy may vary.

### Recommendation
Use **Option A** when precision matters (e.g., publication, legal clarity, archival documentation).  
Use **Option B** when convenience matters and minor ambiguity is acceptable.

## The prompt
```md
**AI‑Assist Spectrum Rating Prompt**

Use the following spectrum to rate the authorship level of the final work:

- **0** — Fully human; no AI involvement  
- **1** — Human‑authored; AI refined wording or clarity  
- **2** — Human‑authored; AI developed it further  
- **3** — Human‑led; AI co‑created through iteration and selection  
- **4** — AI‑led; human provided constraints  
- **5** — Fully AI‑generated from a minimal prompt  

Based on this spectrum, provide:  
1) the level,  
2) a brief justification tied to the definitions.
```

Notably, if you use the Spectrum often and consistently, most AI systems tend to recognize the pattern, and you can simply say “Evaluate this using the AI‑Assist Spectrum” without repeating the whole framework. It becomes a smooth, almost effortless part of your workflow.
