# MLese &nbsp; *noun* 
**/ˈɛmˌɛlˌiːz/** &nbsp;&nbsp; *em‑el‑eez*

## Overview
MLese is a machine‑oriented dialect that emerges when humans distort natural language to satisfy the perceived constraints, preferences, or parsing behavior of AI systems. It uses the surface syntax of human language, but abandons the pragmatic, contextual, and cognitive patterns that make language human.

The defining property:

    MLese is human language syntactically, but no human would speak that way.

MLese is a linguistic failure mode that arises when communication is optimized for machines rather than humans.

## Definition
MLese &nbsp; *noun*  
A form of communication in which humans write using the vocabulary and grammar of natural language, but shape the text primarily for machine parsing instead of human understanding. MLese prioritizes structural predictability, explicitness, and keyword density over conceptual clarity, narrative flow, and human‑natural expression.

## Characteristics
MLese exhibits consistent surface traits that distinguish it from natural human communication:

- Over‑explicitness — stating details humans would normally leave implicit.

- Mechanical sequencing — rigid step‑by‑step phrasing (“First, next, then, finally…”).

- Keyword repetition — repeating terms to “anchor” the model.

- Template mimicry — unnatural adherence to fixed structures.

- Flattened nuance — absence of ambiguity, hedging, or contextual inference.

- Inhuman redundancy — restating the same idea multiple times.

- Hyper‑specific constraints — instructions like “Use exactly 3 bullet points…”.

These features are not chosen for human clarity; they are artifacts of writing for a parser.

## Why MLese Emerges
MLese appears when humans believe AI systems require:

- maximal explicitness
- rigid structure
- zero ambiguity
- verbose restatement
- keyword‑dense phrasing
- template‑like instructions

This belief — sometimes accurate, often exaggerated — causes humans to contort their communication to suit machine heuristics. MLese is the linguistic equivalent of writing code for the compiler instead of for other humans.

## Consequences
- **Erodes human comprehension** — text becomes harder for humans to skim, reason about, or learn from.

- **Creates a self‑reinforcing loop** — humans write MLese → AI trains on MLese → AI generates MLese → humans imitate it.

- **Collapses meaning into structure** — narrative, intent, and conceptual framing disappear.

- **Shifts burden from machine to human** — humans adapt to machine limitations instead of machines adapting to human intent.

- **Produces brittle documentation** — machine‑optimized text breaks when models change; human‑optimized text does not.

- **Leaks back into day‑to‑day language** — as people repeatedly write in MLese for machine‑facing tasks, its mechanical patterns begin appearing in everyday communication, subtly shifting how humans express themselves.

## Etymology
Formed from ML (“machine learning”) + the suffix ‑ese, used to denote a manner of speaking or writing (as in legalese, bureaucratese).

## Usage Note
MLese commonly appears in:

- prompt engineering
- ATS‑optimized resumes
- documentation written for AI agents
- SEO‑driven content
- machine‑targeted instructions

It is considered a violation of intent‑based communication, which holds that humans should express intent naturally and machines must adapt — not the reverse.
