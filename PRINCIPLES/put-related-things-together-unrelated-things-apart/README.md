# Put Related Things Together, Unrelated Things Apart

<p align="right">Details-expanded with assistance of AI tools</p>

## What This Principle Means
Meaningful structure emerges when related elements are grouped and unrelated elements are separated. It applies across all scales of work—from code formatting to system architecture to conceptual reasoning. Grouping reduces cognitive load, separation prevents accidental coupling, and together they create clarity.

## Why It Matters
- Grouping related elements creates coherence and reveals structure.  
- Separating unrelated elements prevents confusion and false relationships.  
- The symmetry of the rule covers both major failure modes of design: scattering what belongs together and mixing what should be apart.  
- This principle aligns with how humans naturally perceive, chunk, and navigate information.  
- It is domain‑agnostic: it applies equally to code, design, writing, architecture, and conceptual thinking.

## How It Shows Up in Practice
1. **Proximity implies relationship; separation implies distinction.**  
2. **Scattering related elements increases cognitive load and hides structure.**  
3. **Mixing unrelated elements creates accidental coupling and conceptual noise.**  
4. **Grouping and separation together define the full space of structural clarity.**  
5. **The principle scales: it applies at the level of lines, functions, modules, systems, and ideas.**  
6. **The symmetry is essential; each half guards against a different failure mode.**  
7. **This is not a formatting preference but a cognitive and architectural invariant.**
